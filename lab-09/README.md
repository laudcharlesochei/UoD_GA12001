
# Practical Manual  
## Lab 09: CRM Sales Pipeline Simulation & Customer Process Mapping

---

## 1. Lab Overview

Customer relationships are at the heart of digital business. Many organisations use **CRM (Customer Relationship Management)** systems to manage customer data, track sales activity, and improve customer experience.

In this lab, you will:

- Understand how CRM systems support **customer-facing business processes**
- Simulate a **sales pipeline** using a real CRM interface (Pipedrive)
- Create a **customer process map** from enquiry to conversion
- Identify **where CRM automation and data** can improve business performance

This lab focuses on **business understanding and decision-making**, not technical configuration or coding.

---

## 2. Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

- Explain the role of CRM systems in digital businesses
- Describe common stages of a sales pipeline
- Use a CRM interface to manage **contacts** and **deals**
- Map a customer process and identify **CRM touchpoints** and automation opportunities
- Analyse how CRM could improve operations in your organisation (or a known organisation)

## 6. Step-by-Step Lab Procedure (Student Instructions)

---

### Part A: CRM Concepts & Sales Pipeline (10 minutes)

#### Step 1: Review CRM fundamentals
Quick reminders:

- **CRM** = customer-facing system (sales, customer service, marketing)  
- **ERP** = internal operations system (inventory, finance, HR, procurement)

Common CRM uses:
- Sales tracking
- Customer communication history
- Reporting and forecasting

#### Step 2: Understand a sales pipeline
Write these typical pipeline stages at the top of your notes or worksheet:

1. Lead captured  
2. Contacted  
3. Qualified  
4. Proposal sent  
5. Deal won / lost  

---

### Part B: CRM Sales Pipeline Simulation in Pipedrive (30–40 minutes)

> ⚠️ No coding required. You are using Pipedrive through the web interface only.

#### Step 3: Access Pipedrive
Choose one option:

- **Option A: Free trial account**  
  Go to Pipedrive and start the free trial using your email address.

- **Option B: Demo access (if provided)**  
  Use the login details or link shared in class.

Once logged in, find:
- **Contacts** (people/organisations)
- **Deals** (pipeline board / sales progress)

---

#### Step 4: Create 3 fictional customer contacts (no real personal data)

1. In the left menu, click **Contacts**
2. Click **Add person** (or **+** button)
3. Create **three fictional contacts**, each with:
   - Name (fictional)
   - Company/Organisation (fictional)
   - Email (use placeholder format)
   - Industry (add in notes if no field exists)

Use these examples (or create your own):

**Contact 1**
- Name: Alex Chen  
- Company: TechNova Inc.  
- Email: alex.chen@technova.example.com  
- Industry: Technology  

**Contact 2**
- Name: Jamie Rivera  
- Company: Bloom Boutique  
- Email: jamie.rivera@bloomboutique.example.com  
- Industry: Retail  

**Contact 3**
- Name: Sam Fischer  
- Company: DataSecure LLC  
- Email: sam.fischer@datasecure.example.com  
- Industry: Cybersecurity  

**Purpose:** You are practising how CRMs capture and store customer data.

---

#### Step 5: Create a new pipeline called **SalesDemo** (detailed steps)

Creating a new pipeline keeps your work separate from any default pipeline.

##### 5.1 Open Pipeline Settings
1. In the top-right, click your **profile icon** (picture or initials)
2. Click **Settings**
3. In the left menu, go to:  
   **Deal settings → Pipelines**

##### 5.2 Add a new pipeline
4. Click **+ Add pipeline**
5. Set:
   - **Pipeline name:** `SalesDemo`
   - **Deal probability:** keep **Based on stage** (default)
   - **Access:** choose **All users** (if the option appears)
6. Click **Continue**

##### 5.3 Add stages to the SalesDemo pipeline
7. You will now add stages. If an unwanted default stage appears, delete it (trash icon), then add your own stages.

Create stages in this order (use the suggested probabilities):

1. **New Lead** (10%)  
2. **Initial Contact** (20%)  
3. **Needs Identified** (50%)  
4. **Proposal Sent** (75%)  
5. **Negotiation** *(optional but realistic)* (90%)  
6. **Closed Won** (100%)  
7. **Closed Lost** (0%)

8. Click **Finish / Done** to save.

##### 5.4 Switch to your new pipeline
9. Return to **Deals**
10. At the top of the deals board, use the pipeline dropdown to select **SalesDemo**

> Tip: A common mistake is creating the pipeline but forgetting to switch to it in **Deals**.

---

#### Step 6: Add sample deals and move them through stages (expanded)

**Before you start:** Make sure you are viewing the **SalesDemo** pipeline in **Deals**.

You will create **three deals**, then simulate progress by dragging the deal cards between stages.

---

   - **Stage:** New Lead

##### Deal 3: “Network Security Audit & Compliance” (for Sam Fischer)

1. Click **+ Add deal**
2. Fill in:
   - **Deal name:** Network Security Audit & Compliance  
   - **Contact:** Sam Fischer  
   - **Organisation:** DataSecure LLC

---

##### Move deals through the pipeline (simulate progress)

1. **Move Deal 1** (E-Commerce Platform Upgrade) → **Initial Contact**  
   - Drag the deal card from **New Lead** to **Initial Contact**

2. **Move Deal 2** (Marketing Support Package) → **Needs Identified**  
   - Drag the deal card to **Needs Identified**
   - Click the deal card → find **Notes** → add:
     - “Spoke with Jamie. Main need is increasing local store traffic. Proposal should focus on local SEO + Google Business Profile.”

3. **Move Deal 3** (Security Audit) → **Proposal Sent**  
   - Drag the deal card to **Proposal Sent**
   - Click the deal card → find **Activities** → **Add activity**
     - Type: Email
     - Subject: “Proposal & Quote for Security Audit”
     - Due date: set to yesterday (to simulate it was sent)
     - Mark as done (if available)

---

##### Close a deal (Won or Lost)

4. **Close Deal 3 as Won**
   - Drag “Network Security Audit & Compliance” to **Closed Won**
   - Confirm the close date
   - Click **Mark as won**

5. *(Optional)* Close Deal 2 as Lost
   - Drag “Annual Marketing Support Package” to **Closed Lost**
   - Choose a loss reason if prompted (e.g., Budget, Competitor)
   - Click **Mark as lost**

---

##### Observe insights and forecasting (what to look for)

- At the top of each stage column, notice:
  - Number of deals in the stage
  - Total value in the stage

To view forecasting:
1. Click **Insights**
2. Find **Forecast**
3. Make sure the pipeline selector is set to **SalesDemo**

**Key idea:** Forecasting often uses *stage probability*.  
Example: If a £25,000 deal is in **Initial Contact** (20%), it contributes **£5,000** to the weighted forecast.

### Part C: Customer Process Mapping Workshop (20–25 minutes)

#### Step 8: Open a process-mapping tool
Choose one:
- **Draw.io (diagrams.net)** *(recommended: no login)*  
- **Lucidchart** (free tier)

#### Step 9: Map a customer sales process (use swimlanes)
Create a swimlane diagram with three lanes:

- **Customer**
- **Sales staff**
- **CRM system (Pipedrive)**

Use a flow similar to this:

Customer submits enquiry →  
Sales receives lead →  
CRM records contact & deal →  
Sales contacts customer →  
Proposal created →  
Deal closed (won/lost)

> Tip: Use simple boxes and arrows. Clarity matters more than artistic design.

#### Step 10: Highlight CRM touchpoints
On your diagram, clearly mark:

**Where data is entered into the CRM**
- creating contact/deal
- updating stage
- adding notes
- attaching documents
- closing won/lost

**Where automation could help**
- automated welcome emails
- follow-up reminders
- manager alerts for high-value deals
- automated reporting

You can use symbols if you like, for example:
- Data entry: **[DB]**
- Automation: **🤖**
